I'm a big believer in the benefits of [Continuous Integration](http://www.martinfowler.com/articles/continuousIntegration.html), and so I have become a user and then contributor of [CruiseControl](http://cruisecontrol.sourceforge.net/).

I find the continually increasing awareness of CruiseControl in the development community quite exciting. [Mike Clark's new book](http://www.pragmaticprogrammer.com/sk/auto/ "Pragmatic Project Automation") has been receiving [a](http://www.pragmaticautomation.com/cgi-bin/pragauto.cgi "Pragmatic Automation") [lot](http://developers.slashdot.org/article.pl?sid=04/08/26/1550255 "Getting Your Boss to Buy Lava Lamps") [of](http://pragprog.com/pragdave/Random/TopThousand.rdoc "Cracking the Top One Thousand") [attention](http://www.theserverside.com/news/thread.tss?thread_id=29271 "Book Review: Pragmatic Project Automation") , which has been translating directly into a lot of new users. I find the [CruiseControl project statistics](http://sourceforge.net/project/stats/index.php?report=months&group_id=23523) graph at SourceForge very interesting indeed. I expect that this month will break page view and download records again, especially if version 2.2 gets released on time.

Why am I excited about this? Besides the fact that Continuous Integration is a fantastic development process, I also have a personal stake in CruiseControl. I have been making contributions to the website and documentation, an area that is traditionally quite weak in open source projects. In helping out and assuming ownership in a relatively unmaintained area, I've become quite connected with the success of the project. Marking out my home in the noosphere, I guess.
