I recently saw a reference to [Devops 101](https://github.com/kgxsz/devops-101) on Twitter. It looked interesting, so I bookmarked it, and forgot about it for a few weeks.

![Devops 101 - Part 4](/2014/12/01/devops-101/images/part-four-goal.png)

Last night I finally got around to trying the exercises - it was a bit of an eye-opener. There's a huge amount of functionality on AWS that I'm not really up-to-date with (and I say this as a person who has been running an AWS EC2 instance for many years now).

The "101" part of the name implies that it's a starting place for graduate developers. I would clarify to say that it's very useful for _anyone_ who isn't working on infrastructure-as-code day to day. If you're developer who is not yet embedded in a team working devopsly, give it a go.

I had never had a chance to place with the AWS command line, and leveraging that with [cloudformation](http://aws.amazon.com/cloudformation/) and [ansible](https://github.com/ansible/ansible) was an real pleasure to use.

I previously worked at a company where we had built our own [stack builder](https://github.com/tim-group/stackbuilder) tool. I never got to grips with it at the time, but I now want to go over it again.
